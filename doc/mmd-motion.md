# MMD motion and pose readers (issue #101, first to fourth slice)

`rust/src/mmd_motion/` reads VMD motions and VPD poses and provides the retargeting data and maths for the 19-bone
standard depth skeleton. `rig.apply_mmd_pose` (second slice, below) writes one pose into one parameter keypoint, and
`rig.bake_mmd_motion` (third slice) samples a motion into several keypoints and an animation lane. The fourth slice
adds a per-name override of the bone table, which can be stored with the document, and a morph to parameter mapping
for the bake. The VMD itself is never played back per frame.

No real VMD or VPD file was read. The formats are implemented from their commonly documented layouts, and every test
builds its streams with a small writer. Treat byte offsets as unverified until a real file has been parsed.

## What is read

- **VMD**: header (`Vocaloid Motion Data 0002` with a 20-byte model name, or the old `Vocaloid Motion Data file`
  with 10 bytes), bone keys (111 bytes), morph keys (23 bytes). Camera, light, shadow and IK sections are skipped
  after their counts are checked against the remaining bytes; their entry counts are reported.
- **VPD**: Shift-JIS text, `Bone<n>{name`, `x,y,z;`, `x,y,z,w;`, `}`, with `//` comments. Text after the declared
  bone blocks is ignored.
- **Names** are Shift-JIS with the Windows code page 932 (CP932) mapping. The decoder reads ASCII, half-width
  katakana and the 9604 double-byte pairs that Python's `cp932` codec maps: JIS X 0208 (all kanji), NEC row 13, the
  NEC-selected IBM extensions, the IBM extensions, and the user-defined area 0xF040 to 0xF9FC (read as the private
  use area, as the codec does). The table is `rust/src/mmd_motion/sjis_table.rs`, generated by
  `python3 tools/gen-sjis-table.py`; `python3 tools/gen-sjis-table.py --check` fails when it is stale, and a test
  reads every pair back through the decoder against the codec's count and checksum.
  Anything else (a pair CP932 leaves unmapped, a lone lead byte, 0x80, 0xA0, 0xFD to 0xFF) becomes U+FFFD, and the
  name keeps its raw bytes (`MmdName::raw`, `hex()`). Nothing encodes text to Shift-JIS.

## Limits

VMD at most 64 MiB, frame number at most 1,000,000 (30 fps, about 9 hours). VPD at most 1 MiB, 4096 bones, and 128
bytes per bone name or model file name; only the lines the declared bones need are collected. Counts are checked
against the remaining bytes before any allocation. Non-finite floats are refused. Quaternions are normalised. A
quaternion of exactly (0, 0, 0, 0) is read as the identity and counted (`zero_quaternions` on `Vmd`, `Vpd` and the
report); a non-zero quaternion shorter than 1e-6 is refused. Bezier control bytes above 127 are clamped. Errors name
a key index and at most 16 characters of a name with control characters replaced by `?` (VPD errors name the bone
index only), never a path or file content.

## Interpolation layout (VMD)

The block holds 16 control bytes `X_x1 Y_x1 Z_x1 R_x1 | X_y1 .. | X_x2 .. | X_y2 ..` (element `4k + c` is control
value `k`, 0 x1, 1 y1, 2 x2, 3 y2, of channel `c`, 0 X, 1 Y, 2 Z, 3 rotation) four times; row `r` (at byte `16r`) is the
sequence shifted left by `r` and padded with `01 00 00`. Newer MMD overwrites bytes 2 and 3 with a physics flag
(`0x63 0x0F`). The reader takes control `k` of channel `c` from the first element of its own row, byte `16c + 4k`,
divided by 127. On a clean block that equals byte `4k + c`, and it never touches bytes 2 and 3 (tested with a full
clean block and with the flag set). The raw 64 bytes stay on each key. A curve eases the segment ending at that key. Equal control points on the diagonal
give exact linear output; otherwise the x parameter is found by 40 bisection steps.

## Coordinates

Rig: x screen right, y down (`skeleton.rs` puts the head at the smaller y), z toward the viewer (`depth_draw` treats
the larger depth as the front). `depth_edit_rotation` builds `Rz(roll) Ry(-yaw) Rx(-pitch)`.
MMD: x right, y up, z away from the camera (the model faces -z).

The change of basis is `P = diag(1, -1, -1)` (determinant +1). A rotation `R` becomes `P R P^T`, so a quaternion
(x, y, z, w) becomes rig (x, -y, -z, w), and a position (x, y, z) becomes (x, -y, -z). Pitch, yaw and roll come from
the rig's own `euler_from_depth_edit`.

Two functions use this, and they answer different questions. `mmd_quaternion_to_rig_world_pitch_yaw_roll` gives the
angles of a rotation written on the rig's world axes (30 degrees about MMD x gives pitch -30, about y yaw +30, about
z roll -30). That is correct only for a bone whose rest orientation is the identity. A bone pose acts in the bone's
rest frame: `bone_deform` builds `rest = quat_from_unit(+y, tail - head) * roll` and poses a bone as
`world = world_prefix * pose`, where the prefix of a bone with unposed ancestors is its rest (a posed parent adds its
own rotation to the prefix, so a child's pose stays parent-relative). The pose a bone needs for the world rotation
`D = P q P` is therefore `rest^-1 * D * rest`, which `mmd_quaternion_to_bone_pose(rest, q)` computes (`rest` is the
`[w, x, y, z]` rest orientation). For an upright bone (Pelvis, Spine, Chest, Neck, Head; tail - head = -y) the rest is
a 180 degree turn about z, so 30 degrees about MMD x gives pitch +30, about y yaw -30, about z roll -30. For a bone
pointing to +x the rest is -90 degrees about z and pitch and yaw swap: x gives yaw -30, y gives pitch -30, z roll -30;
for a bone pointing to -x, x gives yaw +30, y pitch +30, z roll -30. A test pushes random rotations through the
rig's own `rest * pose` path and compares the result with `P q P` to 1e-9. This assumes an MMD bone rotation acts on
model axes (no per-bone local axis, which the MMD format does not carry for standard bones).

The gimbal case of this parametrisation is at yaw of +-90 degrees (yaw is the middle angle), not pitch. There the
rig's rule applies: roll 0, the coupled angle in pitch.

Assumption not verified against a real file: a VMD quaternion acts on MMD coordinates as the standard rotation matrix
of (x, y, z, w).

Left and right follow the picture, not the letter. `skeleton.rs` puts `.L` bones at negative x and MMD 左 bones are

(左 to `.L`) mirrors the motion on screen unless x is also mirrored.

Each rig bone channel has one source. The report (`RetargetReport`) lists mapped bones, unmapped bones and morphs
(decoded text, raw bytes in hex, and whether the name is a recognised standard name) and the frame range.

### Bone table override: `boneTable`

Both operations take `boneTable`, an object from MMD bone name to a rig `boneId` or `null`, at most 64 entries:

```json
{"boneTable": {"上半身3": "Chest", "上半身2": null, "頭": null}, "storeBoneTable": true}
```

- A `boneId` (one of the 19 names of the table above, such as `"Chest"` or `"UpperArm.R"`) makes a name **outside**
  the table drive that bone's rotation. `null` takes a name out: the file's bone is treated as absent (the MMD
  identity, as for a file that does not name it) and listed in `unmappedBones`.
- An entry for a name the file does not hold is not an error. A standard name mapped to its own bone changes nothing.
- What an override means for the hierarchy compensation: the name is read in the place of the rig bone's standard
  MMD counterpart, and nothing else changes. `上半身3: "Chest"` is read as if the file had called that bone 上半身2,
  with 上半身2's parent chain (センター, 上半身), so the Head, which takes the whole chain, follows it. The maths of
  "Hierarchy compensation" below runs on the same inputs it would get from a file that used the standard name, so
  its result is as correct as the statement the entry makes: that the bone sits in the model where the counterpart
  sits. Neither a VMD nor a VPD holds the model's hierarchy, so that statement cannot be checked.
- Refused, each with a message that names the entry:
  - a target that is not one of the 19 rig bones (`names an unknown rig bone`);
  - a standard name (any name of the table above, the unmapped ones included) mapped to another bone
    (`redirects a standard MMD bone`). The compensation already knows where 頭 or センター sits, or that 腰 and
    左足ＩＫ have no place in the rig's chain, and reading one under another bone's name would contradict that. This
    rules out a left and right swap by table and using グルーブ or 腰 for the Pelvis;
  - two names for one rig bone, the standard one included (`maps to Head, which "頭" already drives`). To replace
    頭, map 頭 to `null` in the same table;
  - a value that is neither a string nor `null`, more than 64 entries, and a name that is empty, longer than 384
    UTF-8 bytes (3 for each of the 128 Shift-JIS bytes that a VPD name may hold), or holds a control character or
    U+FFFD.
- A table targets rotations only. The Pelvis translation always comes from センター, which can be taken out but not
  replaced; an entry for `"Pelvis"` replaces 下半身.
- Names are matched as decoded text. A name with bytes the decoder does not read (see "What is read"; a pair that
  CP932 leaves unmapped) holds U+FFFD and cannot be a key, so such a bone cannot be redirected.
  CP932 has pairs that decode to the same character (0x81BE and the NEC row 13 pair 0x879C are both U+222A). Two
  names in one file that differ only in such pairs are one name here: for a bone a VMD uses the track that comes
  first in byte order and a VPD the bone listed last, and for a morph the keys of both are read as one track. Not seen
  in a real file.
- The report's `mapped[].mmd` spells a name as the file does, and `boneTable` says which table applied:
  `"argument"`, `"document"` or `"default"`.

**Stored with the document.** `storeBoneTable: true` (it needs `boneTable`) saves the table with the pose or bake in
the same undo step, also when the pose itself changes nothing; `dryRun` stores nothing. It is kept as the JSON of the
argument in the INX extension section under `io.github.geekjapan.soul2d.MmdBoneTable`, the mechanism the flip pairs
use, so it is written by `save`, read back by `open`, and visible to `extdata.get`. A stored table is the default of
every later call on any rig of the document; a `boneTable` argument replaces it for that call (`{}` gives the plain
default) and is not merged with it. Storing `{}` removes the entry. The stored table is file content: it is read up
to 64 KiB (the largest table the argument accepts takes about 50 KiB) and checked like the argument, and one that fails refuses the call until a `boneTable` is passed. It holds
bone names, not node ids, so it does not depend on the rig it was stored with.

## Applying a pose: `rig.apply_mmd_pose`

MCP tool `rig_apply_mmd_pose`, Session operation `rig.apply_mmd_pose`:

```json
{"op": "rig.apply_mmd_pose", "root": 3, "parameter": 22, "path": "pose.vpd",
 "key": [1, 1], "frame": 5, "translationScale": 31.5, "boneTable": {"頭": null}, "storeBoneTable": false,
 "dryRun": false}
```

- `root` (DepthRigRoot) and `parameter` are required. `path` ends in `.vpd` or `.vmd` (case-insensitive). `frame` is
  required for `.vmd` (a number from 0 to 1000000, fractional allowed; the track of each mapped bone is sampled with
  `sample_bone`) and refused for `.vpd`.
- `key` is an explicit `[x, y]` keypoint of the parameter, as `binding.set_trs` takes it. Without it the keypoint
  closest to the parameter's current value is used, as `rig.drag_bone` and `rig.apply_deform` do. A `key` that does not
  exist is refused before the file is read.
- `boneTable` and `storeBoneTable` are described under "Bone table override". A table is checked before the file is
  read. Morphs are only reported here: a pose is written into a keypoint, which has no place for a morph weight.
- Writing goes through the `binding.set_trs` path: `transform.r.x/y/z` (pitch, yaw, roll, radians) and, for the
  Pelvis, `transform.t.x/y/z` are set at that keypoint of `parameter`, missing bindings are created, and the keypoint
  deform is refreshed by the same dirty flush (`dirty::mark_pose`). One undo step. Cells
  that were never set are interpolated by the binding, as for any pose edit.
- Applying a pose replaces the whole pose of the keypoint: every standard-skeleton bone the name table can drive is
  written, and a bone the file does not name counts as the MMD identity, so its computed rig pose (zero, or what undoes
  its parent, as Spine after a 下半身 turn) is written too. A zero (a bone at the identity, or the Pelvis translation when the file does not name センター) is
  written only into a channel this parameter already drives (a zero channel of a named bone included), so the pose does not depend on what the keypoint held
  before and the parameter gains no binding for a bone the pose leaves alone. Extra rig
  bones outside the table are not touched. A pose that names センター creates the Pelvis `transform.t.*` bindings in `parameter`
  (see the note under "Translation scale"). The write is exact: a value that differs from the stored one by
  any amount is stored as reported.
- `dryRun: true` computes everything, including the binding writes on a copy, and returns the report; the document,
  revision and history are untouched. The report is the same with and without `dryRun`.
- The file is read like the other importers (`read_bounded_file`): VPD up to 1 MiB, VMD up to 64 MiB, errors never
  carry the path. Native CMO3/CAN3 documents refuse the operation like any other `rig.*` operation.

The report lists `mapped` (per rig bone: the MMD names that contributed, `pitch`, `yaw`, `roll`, or the translation
`value`, and `namedInFile`: true when the file names the bone, false when the angles were computed only to undo a
parent), `reset` (bones not named and at the identity that this parameter already drives, written as zero: `rig`, `bone`, `channel`), `unmappedBones` and `unmappedMorphs` (decoded text with control characters replaced, `hex`, `standard`; at
most 256 each, with exact `...Count`), `missingInRig` (a mapped bone this rig lacks), `locked` (a bone with
`lockRotation` or `lockTranslation` that the file names, or whose computed turn to undo a parent was skipped), `zeroQuaternions`, `frame`, `key`, `translationApplied`,
`translationScale` and `boneTable` (`"argument"`, `"document"` or `"default"`). A bone with `lockRotation` is never written because the deform path ignores its pose.

### Hierarchy compensation

The rig poses a bone as `world = parent_world * rest_parent^-1 * rest * pose`, or `rest * pose` when the bone ignores
its parent (`lockToRoot` or `allowParentToTargets: false`; the standard skeleton does this for Head and both Feet).
Let `D = world * rest^-1`, a rotation on world axes: then `D(bone) = D(parent) * L` with `L = rest * pose * rest^-1`.
MMD wants `D(bone)` to be the product of the file's parent-relative rotations from センター down (`mmd_world`, with
the rig conjugation of "Coordinates", which commutes with products). So the plan solves

    L = D(rig parent)^-1 * mmd_world(bone),   pose = rest^-1 * L * rest   (mmd_quaternion_to_bone_pose)

where `D(rig parent)` is the world rotation the plan already gave the rig parent (identity for a bone that ignores its
parent). Where the rig and MMD parents agree this is the file's own rotation. The one standard-skeleton mismatch is
Spine: its rig parent Pelvis carries `センター * 下半身`, but 上半身 hangs under センター only, so
`L(Spine) = (センター 下半身)^-1 (センター 上半身) = 下半身^-1 * 上半身`, and Pelvis gets `センター * 下半身`. Chest, Neck,
Clavicles and Thighs need nothing extra. Head and Feet ignore their parent in the rig, so they take the whole MMD chain.
A bone the file does not name is written too: with its `L` when that is not the identity (Spine after a 下半身 turn,
Feet after any ancestor turn, Head after a 上半身, 上半身2 or 首 turn), otherwise as zero when this parameter already drives it (`reset` in the report). The test `every_chain_reaches_the_mmd_world_rotation`
(`apply_tests.rs`) poses the rig through its own forward path (`build_runtime_limited`) and compares each bone's world
rotation with the MMD one to 1e-6.

### Translation scale

センター translation goes to the Pelvis `transform.t.*` as `(x, -y, -z) * scale`, scale in rig units per MMD unit.
Default scale: the rig's Pelvis-to-Head rest distance divided by `NOMINAL_MMD_PELVIS_TO_HEAD = 8.0` MMD units. That
constant is an assumption, not a measurement: MMD models are about 8 cm per unit, so a 1.58 m figure is about 20
units and its 下半身 to 頭 span about 0.64 m. It has not been checked on a real model. `translationScale` (finite,
positive) overrides it. Without a Pelvis and Head, or with a zero span, there is no default and the translation is
not applied (`translationApplied: false`). グルーブ and 全ての親 are not added to the Pelvis.

The Pelvis `t.*` are written when the file names センター, and reset to zero when it does not but this parameter already holds them. Writing them into a parameter other than the one that already drives them gives those channels two owning
parameters, after which `rig.drag_bone` refuses the Pelvis (no single translation parameter). Pick the parameter
that owns the Pelvis translation, or accept that.

## Baking a motion: `rig.bake_mmd_motion`

MCP tool `rig_bake_mmd_motion`, Session operation `rig.bake_mmd_motion`:

```json
{"op": "rig.bake_mmd_motion", "root": 3, "parameter": 22, "path": "walk.vmd", "frames": [0, 10, 30],
 "axis": 0, "other": 0, "animation": "Walk", "translationScale": 31.5,
 "morphs": {"まばたき": {"parameter": 31, "axis": 0, "range": [1.0, 0.0]}},
 "boneTable": {"頭": null}, "storeBoneTable": false, "dryRun": false}
```

The rig is posed through parameter keypoints and an animation moves a parameter over time, so a motion is baked as
"poses at keypoints along one parameter axis" plus "a lane that walks the parameter through those keypoints".

- `root`, `parameter`, `path` (a `.vmd` file) and `frames` are required. `frames` is the one input form: 1 to 256
  strictly increasing whole VMD frame numbers (0 to 1000000, 30 fps). The caller chooses them, so the sampling can be
  dense where the motion moves and sparse where it rests.
- Sample `i` (the pose `rig.apply_mmd_pose` would write for `frame = frames[i]`, planned and written by the same code:
  `plan`, `drop_unowned_resets`, `cells`, `set_pose_cells`) goes into keypoint `i` along `axis` (0 or 1, default 0;
  1 only for a 2D parameter). On a 2D parameter the other axis stays at keypoint `other` (default 0, must exist); a
  1D parameter takes neither `axis: 1` nor `other`. More frames than keypoints on the axis are refused:
  **the parameter must already have enough keypoints**, this operation does not add any
  (`parameter.set_axes` does). Keypoints past the last frame are not touched.
- Ownership is judged over the whole motion, not per frame: a channel that any sampled frame makes nonzero is written
  at every sampled keypoint (zero where that frame leaves it at zero). Without that, a channel that only later frames
  move would keep its unset cell at the earlier keypoints, and unset cells are interpolated from the set ones, so the
  first frames would not reproduce the motion. A channel that stays zero in every frame gets no binding, as in
  `rig.apply_mmd_pose`.
- `animation` (optional, a non-empty name) creates, or **replaces entirely** (its other lanes are dropped), an
  animation with `timestep` 1/30 s and `length` `frames.last - frames.first + 1`, holding one `Forced` lane with
  `Linear` interpolation on `(parameter, axis)`. Its keyframes sit at `frames[i] - frames[0]` with the parameter value
  of keypoint `i` (`min + (max - min) * axis point`). One animation frame is one VMD frame and the animation starts at
  the first sampled frame, so animation frame `t` plays VMD frame `frames[0] + t`. Both the lane and the binding
  interpolate linearly, so between two sampled frames the rig shows a straight blend of the two baked poses, not the
  VMD's own curve: sample densely where the motion has fast or curved parts. A frame outside the motion is held at the
  nearest key, as in `sample_bone`. The lane moves one axis only; on a 2D parameter the other axis keeps its current
  runtime value, which reproduces the motion only while it sits on keypoint `other`.
- `boneTable` and `storeBoneTable` work as in `rig.apply_mmd_pose` ("Bone table override"); the table is stored in
  the bake's undo step.
- One undo step, covering the keypoint writes, the deform refresh of every written keypoint and the animation.
  `dryRun: true` computes everything on a copy and returns the report; the document, revision and history are
  untouched, and the report is the same with and without `dryRun`. Inputs are validated before the file is read; the
  file is read like `rig.apply_mmd_pose` (VMD up to 64 MiB, errors without paths). A refusal changes nothing.
- The report holds `frames` (one `{frame, key, namedBones, translationApplied, locked}` entry per sample),
  `keypointsWritten`, `axis`, `other`, `animation` (`{name, length, timestep}` or `null`), `motionFrameRange`,
  `unmappedBoneCount`, `unmappedMorphCount`, the unions `missingInRig` and `locked`, `zeroQuaternions`,
  `translationScale`, `boneTable` and `morphs`. It carries no per-bone lists, so its size grows with the number of
  frames (times the number of mapped morphs) only; use `rig.apply_mmd_pose` with `dryRun` for the named unmapped bones
  and morphs and the per-bone angles of one frame.

### Morphs: `morphs`

A morph weight is one number over time, not a pose, so it cannot live in the keypoints the bake writes. What already
plays a number over time is an animation lane, and the bake already writes an animation. `morphs` maps MMD morph
names to parameter axes, and each mapped morph that the file holds becomes one more lane of the baked animation:

```json
{"morphs": {"まばたき": {"parameter": 31, "axis": 0, "range": [1.0, 0.0]}, "あ": {"parameter": 32}}}
```

- At most 64 entries. `parameter` (required) is the id of an existing parameter, `axis` is 0 (default) or 1 (a 2D
  parameter only), and `range` is `[value at weight 0, value at weight 1]`, two finite numbers, default that axis's
  `[min, max]`. A range may run downwards, as for a blink that drives an "eye open" parameter. The value is
  `range[0] + (range[1] - range[0]) * weight` and is clamped neither to 0..1 nor to the parameter's range, like a key
  written with `animation.key.set`.
- `morphs` needs `animation` (refused without it): the lanes are the only place the values go. The parameter the
  motion itself is baked on cannot be a target on either axis (a lane on its other axis would move it off keypoint
  `other`, where the baked poses are), and no two morphs can share a parameter axis.
- A VMD morph key is a name, a frame and a weight, with no curve, so `sample_morph` interpolates linearly between two
  keys and holds the first and last key outside them (keys sorted by frame, the last key of a duplicated frame wins).
  There is no Bezier segment to reproduce for a morph.
- The weight is sampled at the same `frames` as the poses, and the lane (`Forced`, `Linear`) gets one keyframe per
  sampled frame at `frames[i] - frames[0]`. A change between two sampled frames, such as a whole blink, is therefore
  lost, exactly as a pose between two samples is; sample where the morph has its keys. Upgrade path: key the lane at
  the morph's own keys inside the baked range, which would be exact because both sides are linear.
- The report lists one entry per mapping in name order: `{morph, parameter, axis, range, inFile, values}`, `values`
  being the lane values at each of `frames`. A mapped name that the file does not hold is reported with
  `inFile: false` and `values: null` and gets no lane. A mapped morph is no longer counted in `unmappedMorphCount`;
  the others are, as before.
- Names are matched as decoded text, with the same limit as bone table keys: a morph whose name holds a character
  the decoder does not read (a pair CP932 leaves unmapped) cannot be mapped.
- There is no default morph table, because Soul2D has no standard face parameter list to map onto, and the mapping
  is not stored with the document: it is given with each bake.

Limits specific to baking: no automatic choice of frames (no evenly spaced or per-key-frame form), no keypoint
creation, no looping or retiming (the animation is always 30 fps), and the lanes are linear only. The tests build their
VMD streams with a small writer; no real file was baked.

The report of the last `rig.apply_mmd_pose` or `rig.bake_mmd_motion` in the session, dry run or not, is readable at
`resource://soul2d/rig/mmd-report` (null before the first one).

## Not done yet

- Playback straight from a VMD. The maintainer chose the bake (`rig.bake_mmd_motion`) as the motion representation on
  2026-10-18 (#101), so a motion is baked at caller-chosen frames, not evaluated per frame, and the baked animation is
  linear between the samples.
- Baking several parameter axes at once (for example one lane per bone group), choosing the frames automatically,
  and creating the keypoints a motion needs.
- A default morph table (there is no standard face parameter list yet), a stored morph mapping, morph lanes keyed at
  the morph's own keys, and morphs for `rig.apply_mmd_pose`.
- IK bones, toe bones, eyes, twist bones, fingers, camera, light and shadow.
- A real VMD or VPD file has not been read; byte layouts and the quaternion convention are unverified.
- In the bone table override: redirecting a standard name (so no left and right swap), another source for the Pelvis
  translation than センター, keys for names the decoder cannot read, and an editor for the stored table.
- A custom `DepthBone` inserted between two standard bones (Pelvis, Helper, Spine): the compensation looks only at a
  standard bone's direct parent, so the Spine is not turned back and the torso follows the Pelvis.
- A pose made only of identity rotations on a parameter with no rig binding stores nothing (the parameter already
  gives that pose), so no keypoint deform is created by that apply.
